Abstract

3D mapping of large-scale outdoor environments from LiDAR measurements is a fundamental challenge in computer vision and robotics. While neural implicit representations provide memory-efficient architectures for surface mapping, recovering high-fidelity geometry from sparse point clouds remains susceptible to geometric jitter and high-frequency artifacts. Classical geometry processing suppresses such artifacts with curvature energies whose minimizers define smooth surfaces, and level-set formulations of these energies are well established. In this work, we propose LiDAR mapping as an energy optimization problem, where we derive smooth LiDAR maps by optimizing the variational energy defined over the surface curvature represented by neural implicit representations. We show that this approach enables smooth reconstruction of million-point LiDAR sweeps that observe large open surfaces spanning hundreds of metres. Experiments on large-scale outdoor datasets show that this recovers geometrically consistent surfaces with no additional inference-time penalties.

Method

Energy over the curvature of the level set

A LiDAR sweep observes surfaces, so we smooth the surface rather than the ambient field. For a neural SDF \(u\) with unit normal \(n = \nabla u / \|\nabla u\|\) and tangent projector \(P = I - nn^\top\), the shape operator and its energy are

$$S = \frac{P\,\mathcal{H}(u)\,P}{\|\nabla u\|}, \qquad E_S(u) = \int_\Sigma \|S\|_F \, dA,$$

where the eigenvalues of \(S\) are the principal curvatures, so \(\|S\|_F^2 = \kappa_1^2 + \kappa_2^2\). Unlike a raw Hessian penalty, \(E_S\) charges only for genuine bending: it ignores normal-direction curvature and changes in \(\|\nabla u\|\). A gradient–Hessian alignment term covers exactly that complementary block, so the two never double-count, and a minimal-surface term \(E_M\) penalizes spurious area:

$$\mathcal{L} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{bce}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Eikonal}} + \lambda_S E_S + \lambda_A E_A + \lambda_M E_M .$$

The energy changes only the training objective. Architecture, sampling and inference are those of SHINE-Mapping, so there is no inference-time cost.
